# Data Problems

Line numbers are CSV file lines (the header is line 1).

Sources:

- applications.csv: Airtable, maintained by staff.
- weekly_reflections.csv: a form participants fill in after each session.
- attendance.csv: typed in by facilitators.
- payments.csv: a Stripe export, with Wise bank transfers added by hand.

Most problems come from the hand-maintained parts (Airtable fields, facilitator sheets, manual Wise rows), and from the four sources having no shared key: they can only be joined by email or name. Problems that change refund decisions come first.

| Problem | Example (file and row) | Why it matters | What you would do about it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Refunds that are due have not been paid. | Priya (applications.csv line 25), Jonas (22), Amara (20), Hannah (10) and Yuki (2) meet the refund conditions, but payments.csv has no refund for them. | Five participants are owed $850 in total. The deposit only motivates people if completers reliably get it back. | Pay them now. At the end of each cohort, generate the refund list from the data (refund.py) instead of tracking it by hand. |
| Deposit status in Airtable contradicts the payments and the refund rules. | applications.csv line 18: Ben is "Refunded", but payments.csv line 36 shows the deposit only as Paid, and he attended 6/9 sessions. Line 3: Greta is "Pending", but payments.csv line 28 shows Stripe already refunded her $200. Line 2: Yuki's "Payment received" is unticked, but she paid (payments.csv lines 30-31). | Staff act on Airtable. Ben looks settled when his deposit should be kept, and Greta could be refunded twice (her IBAN is in the notes for a bank refund). | Derive deposit status from attendance, reflections and payments instead of typing it. Sync Stripe payments into Airtable instead of ticking a checkbox. |
| A reflection is labelled with the wrong week. | weekly_reflections.csv line 115: Seun's reflection on 2 July is labelled week 4, but week 4 was already submitted on 25 June (line 93), and 2 July falls in week 5. | Seun looks like they missed week 5, so a strict rule check keeps their $50 deposit although they probably met the conditions. | Pre-fill the week in each participant's form link instead of having them pick it. Until then, check duplicate weeks before refusing a refund. |
| Week 12 attendance for Group 84 was never entered. | attendance.csv lines 20-24 have no week 12 value, but all 5 members submitted week 12 reflections on 20-21 Aug (weekly_reflections.csv). | Isabel's refund can't be decided: she has 7.5 sessions and needs 8. | Ask the facilitator (Bashir Lindqvist) for week 12 attendance. Require attendance to be entered after each session. |
| Blank attendance cells mean different things. | attendance.csv line 10: Hannah is blank for weeks 3-12 in Group 82 after moving to Group 83 (line 16). Line 12: Rafa is blank after dropping out. Group 84 week 12 is blank because it wasn't entered. | A blank can mean "session not held", "not in this group" or "not entered". Hannah's two rows show 22% and 78% when she attended 9/9, which could cost her the refund. | Blank should only mean the session hasn't happened. Done sessions should be 0, 0.5 or 1. Record group moves and dropouts on the membership (start week, end week, reason). |
| Text in a numeric attendance column. | attendance.csv line 12: Rafa has "dropped" in week 3. | Totals and scripts break on text, and a dropout isn't recorded anywhere else. | Put it as 0, and add a 0 / 0.5 / 1 dropdown to the facilitator sheet. Record the dropout on the membership. |
| Attendance totals are typed and wrong. | attendance.csv line 19: Seun has 1 in all 9 weeks, but Sessions Attended is 7 and the rate 78%. | Anyone reading the total would think Seun missed two sessions and deny the refund. | Calculate totals with a locked formula. |
| One person has several emails. | applications.csv line 22: Jonas is `jonas.feld@orgbeta.example`, but `jfeld88@mailbox.example` in attendance.csv line 5, payments.csv line 13 and 4 reflections. | Records are joined by email, so Jonas's attendance, payment and reflections go missing and he would wrongly lose his refund. | One email per person. Give facilitators a pre-filled roster and participants pre-filled form links, so nobody types an email. |
| Emails are not normalised. | applications.csv line 25: `Priya.Nair@orgalpha.example`. weekly_reflections.csv lines 32, 101, 150: trailing space. Line 125: typo `priya.nair@orgalpha.exmaple`. | Records don't match, so Priya's reflections look missing. | Trim and lowercase emails on entry. Pre-fill emails instead of typing them. |
| An application has no Unique ID. | applications.csv line 7: Liang Wei. | Liang can't be referenced reliably, and the ID is how staff identify an application. | Make the ID required and generated by Airtable (autonumber or formula field). |
| Duplicate applications are allowed. | applications.csv lines 16 and 24: Daniel Reyes has RW26-027 (For Matching, note "applied twice?") and RW26-003 (Program Completed). | Counts are inflated, and staff may chase a deposit for an application that isn't real. | One application per person per cohort. Airtable can't enforce uniqueness, so add a duplicate-check view or automation; a database can use a unique constraint. |
| Group values are inconsistent. | applications.csv line 4: "Group 81"; line 5: "81"; line 6: "Group 8383" for Noor, who is in Group 83 in attendance.csv. | Grouping and joins fail, and Noor appears to be in a group that doesn't exist. | Make Group a linked-record field to a Groups table in Airtable. |
| Overall Status is out of date. | applications.csv line 12: Rafa is "In Facilitation Round" but dropped out in week 3. Line 2 (and all of Group 84): "In Facilitation Round" after the week 12 session. | Reports show the wrong numbers, and finished participants wait for refunds because they don't look finished. | Update status when a participant drops or a group finishes. Derive "completed" from the final session. |
| Deposit amount filled in for people with no deposit. | applications.csv line 21: Lucia (Waiting List) has 200 with status "Not Applicable". Line 16: duplicate RW26-027 has 50 "Pending" with no payment. | Totals of deposits held are wrong, and money could be refunded that was never paid. | Only create a deposit when a participant is placed in a group. |
| PayPal payments are not recorded anywhere. | applications.csv line 14: Chiara's channel is "Stripe", but her note says "paid via PayPal 22 May". payments.csv has no PayPal rows and no deposit for her. | Her payment can't be verified, and a refund couldn't be sent back through the right channel. | Record every payment, whatever the channel, as a payment record linked to the application. |
| Sensitive data in free-text notes. | applications.csv line 3: Greta's bank IBAN. Line 20: Amara's mental-health information ("mentioned panic attacks on intake call"). | Everyone with access to the base can see bank details and health data. Health data is special-category data under GDPR. | Delete bank details once used. Keep health information only with explicit consent and restricted access. Replace other notes with structured fields (group moves, payments, decline reason, time preference). |
| Rows from an earlier cohort are mixed in. | applications.csv line 13: Emily's RW25-112 (2025 Fall). weekly_reflections.csv lines 2-7: her 2025 reflections, labelled Group 82 (her 2025 group was 44). | 2025 data can be counted toward 2026, e.g. Emily looks like she submitted two reflections a week. | Filter exports by cohort. Link reflections to an application, not just an email. |
| Attendance 'Name' column is confusing. | attendance.csv line 4: "Daniel R."; line 19: "Seun B" for Oluwaseun Bello. | Preferred names don't match applications.csv, so names can't be used to match people. | Rename it to 'Participant Name'. Better: a pre-filled roster so facilitators never type names. |
| Facilitators have no email. | attendance.csv: Imke Vasquez, Tomasz Adeyemi, Lorna Whitfield-Sato and Bashir Lindqvist appear only by name. | They can't be contacted or given accounts, e.g. to ask Bashir for the missing week 12 attendance. | Record facilitators as people with an email, like participants. |
| Manual payment rows don't identify the payer. | payments.csv line 3: "MD Noor R." with no email or name. | Wise rows can only be matched by guessing from a shortened name, which breaks with two people of the same first name and initial. | Record the application ID on every payment row. |
| Manual payment rows have no status or fee. | payments.csv lines 2-4: all Wise rows have a blank Status and Fee. | It isn't clear whether a transfer went through, and costs are under-reported. | Make status required and record the fee (or mark it unknown). |
| Deposit and contribution are told apart only by free text. | payments.csv lines 5-6: "CBT Lab motivational deposit" and "CBT Lab program contribution". Line 34: Tomas's charge just says "CBT Lab". Line 27: "CBT Lab - M. Alvarez". | The refundable amount can't be read reliably. For Tomas, the deposit share of his charge is unknown. | Use separate Stripe products for deposit and contribution, and put the application ID and payment type in the payment metadata (personal payment links). Use the same type values for Wise rows. |
| Payments don't follow the stated process. | The rules say one payment covers deposit and contribution, but payments.csv lines 5-6 are two charges for Anneke; only line 34 (Tomas) is a single combined charge. | Refund logic has to handle both shapes, and a combined charge hides the deposit amount. | Pick one process and record deposit and contribution amounts explicitly. |
| Dates use two formats. | payments.csv line 2: "02/09/2026" (DD/MM/YYYY); line 5: "2026-05-14 16:41". | 02/09/2026 is 2 September but can be read as 9 February. | Store ISO 8601 timestamps in UTC; enter manual rows in Stripe's format. |
| Refunds are recorded in two ways. | payments.csv line 5: Stripe marks the original charge (Amount Refunded 200). Line 2: Wise adds a negative row (-50.00). | Every refund check needs two rules, and one is easy to miss. | Record each refund as its own row linked to the original charge (as load_db.py does). |
| Mixed currencies. | payments.csv line 34: Tomas paid 212.00 EUR; every other row is USD. | Adding up the Amount column gives a wrong total. | Store amount and currency together, and never add up amounts across currencies. |
| Payment with no application. | payments.csv line 38: Marta Kowal paid a $200 deposit but isn't in applications.csv. | $200 is held for someone the programme has no record of, and may be owed back. | Find her application or refund her. Use personal payment links with the application ID, so payments can only be made against an application. |
| Test data in the export. | weekly_reflections.csv line 8: `test@test.example`. | It inflates submission counts and averages. | Remove test submissions, or use a separate test form. |
| Rows typed into the form responses by hand. | weekly_reflections.csv lines 143-144: Yuki and Aisha have rows timestamped exactly 7/13/2026 0:00:00 with all answers blank. | The form records the real submission time, so these weren't submitted. It's unclear what they mean (an excused week?), and they could count as reflections. | Never edit the response sheet. Record exceptions in a separate field, and make week and answers required on the form. |
| Duplicate submissions for the same week. | weekly_reflections.csv lines 47-57: all 5 Group 84 members submitted week 2 twice. Lines 54-55: Aisha's two rows are identical, an hour apart. | Duplicates double-count scores and can hide a missing week. A whole group doing it suggests the week 2 link was sent twice. | One reflection per participant per week (pre-filled link per participant and week), keeping the latest. |
| Participants type their own email and group. | weekly_reflections.csv lines 58, 79, 102, 124: Jonas's reflections under `jfeld88@mailbox.example` have no Group Number. Line 125: Priya's typo email, no group. | These reflections can't be matched to the right person or group. | Pre-fill email and group in each participant's form link, or take the email from their sign-in. |

## Assumptions

- Each group has only 1 facilitator.
- Wellbeing trackers (weeks 1 and 12) are completed by everyone, as they are not in the exports.
- Session ratings in weekly_reflections.csv ("The session today was engaging for me", "The facilitator's behavior supported the session optimally", "I felt seen, supported, and affirmed") are on a 1-10 scale. The form isn't in the exports, and the data only shows values 1-7, so this needs confirming with the team.
